# Four symbols distinguish characteristic two from other fields

For the two-block identity on four symbols, the exact riffle rank is **8 when \(2\ne0\)** and **9 in characteristic two**. The first statement has an end-to-end Lean proof. The characteristic-two obstruction below has a written proof, an independent exhaustive check over \(\mathbb F_2\), and additional finite checks of the normalized geometry over \(\mathbb F_4\) and \(\mathbb F_8\).

All decoder equations use shared first columns \(v_i\), target-dependent matrices \(L,B\), and \(L\operatorname{diag}(x(v_i))B=x(b)I_4\). A change of first-coordinate basis changes both columns and targets. The four targets need only form a basis. The [general lower bound](DYADIC.md) gives at least eight terms over every field.

## Eight signed terms when two is invertible

Use the eight vertices \((1,\pm1,\pm1,\pm1)\). For each of the last three coordinate targets, partition the vertices into four pairs differing only at that coordinate. Half the oriented difference gives the target. For the first coordinate pair opposite triples of signs and take half the sum. Assign the four disjoint recoveries to the four diagonal entries. Lean checks the 256 integral tensor identities before dividing by two in the field. [Exact theorem](FourSymbols.lean)

## A short obstruction over the two-element field

Any nonzero binary functional must be nonzero on at least four columns. Indeed, it takes value one on some basis target and the resulting sum of rank-one matrices must have rank four. Consequently the columns span. Choose four independent columns as a basis and write the width-eight generator as \([I_4\mid P]\). Each row of \(P\) has weight at least three. Each pair of rows differs in at least two positions, by the same distance bound applied to the sum of the two corresponding coordinate functionals. A weight-four row is impossible, because it differs from every other allowable row in at most one position. The four rows have weight three and have distinct zero positions. Permuting columns gives \(P=J-I\).

Thus the columns are exactly the eight odd-parity binary vectors. They form an affine three-space. Some basis target \(b\) has odd parity, so it is one of these points. Every affine plane is the support on the cube of a binary linear functional. The four columns of \(L\) and the four rows of \(B\) on each plane through \(b\) are independent because their product is the identity. Choose a plane away from \(b\). Any two of its points lie together on a plane through \(b\), hence their left vectors are independent and so are their right vectors. On the chosen plane all four vectors on each side are nonzero and distinct. A binary two-space has only three nonzero vectors, so both ranks are at least three. Their product is zero and its middle dimension is four, contradicting the product rank inequality.

The independent checker enumerates all 490,314 unordered multisets of eight first columns, including repeats and zeros in the original target basis. Exactly 15 survive the minimum-distance test. In every survivor, at least one coordinate target has a four-point zero plane on which all six pairs belong to four-term identity supports. The checker also expands all 256 entries of a nine-term tensor-product construction. [Standard-library checker](check_binary_four.py) · [Checks](binary-four-replay.txt)

## Why enlarging a characteristic-two field cannot recover width eight

Extend scalars to an infinite field of characteristic two. We use a rank-equality fact twice. Suppose there are \(2d\) columns away from a target \(b\), and its decoder recovers \(I_d\) with those columns. Choose a functional \(a\) vanishing on \(b\) and nowhere zero on the columns. Write \(H_h=\operatorname{diag}(h(v_i))\). Then

$$U=\operatorname{im}(H_aB)=\ker L,\qquad \dim U=d.$$

For each \(h\) vanishing on \(b\), \(H_hH_a^{-1}\) preserves \(U\). A generic such \(h\) gives distinct diagonal values on the different projective classes of the columns modulo \(b\). Indeed, after normalization by \(a(v_i)\), equality for all such \(h\) is precisely equality in the quotient. Polynomial interpolation in this diagonal operator projects onto each class and preserves \(U\). Hence \(U=\bigoplus_g U_g\) is a sum within the coordinate blocks indexed by the classes. For a functional \(p\) with \(p(b)=1\), \(L H_p H_a^{-1}\) is injective on \(U\). The spaces \(U_g\) and \(H_pH_a^{-1}U_g\) are disjoint and have equal dimension. Therefore \(2\dim U_g\le |g|\). The total dimensions agree, so **each projective class has even size**.

**Seven columns for three independent targets and output size four.** The rectangular bounds for three, two and one targets are seven, six and four. At width seven, on each target line there is at least one column by the diagonal rank inequality, and at most one by quotienting to two targets. A zero column could be discarded and would contradict the lower bound. Projecting away one coordinate leaves an optimal six-column dictionary, in which each remaining target line likewise contains exactly two columns. Thus the original seven columns have exactly one on each singleton support, one on each pair support, and one on the triple support.

Remove the singleton column for a chosen target \(b\). At a detecting functional its contribution has rank at most one, so the remaining matrix has rank at least three. Compose the remaining rows and columns by fixed linear maps that extract \(I_3\) from that matrix. Every functional vanishing at \(b\) still gives zero. The even-class argument now applies with six columns and \(d=3\). In the quotient by \(b\), precisely two columns have both surviving coordinates nonzero. These must be projectively equal. Applying this to each target shows that each pair column is proportional to the restriction of the triple column to its support. Rescale coordinates to make the triple all ones and rescale individual columns. This gives all seven nonzero binary subset indicators, the Fano plane in characteristic two.

**Reconstructing eight first columns.** Suppose now eight columns decode four independent targets. Quotient by any one nonzero column, discarding a target on which its coefficient is nonzero in the target basis. Three independent targets with output size four survive. The seven remaining points must be the Fano configuration just proved. A zero original column is excluded by the universal lower bound. Choose four independent original columns as coordinate axes. In the quotient by any such axis the other three axes form a basis of the Fano configuration. In those coordinates the seven points are binary up to diagonal coordinate scaling and individual column scaling. This follows because any three independent binary points of a Fano plane can be carried to its standard basis by an invertible binary matrix.

No extra column can have support of size at most two, since projection along one of its nonzero coordinates would give zero or duplicate a surviving axis. In each of the four axis quotients exactly three extra columns project to two-element supports. An original support of size three contributes to three such counts and a full support to none. The twelve counts force all four extra supports to have size three, each omitting a different axis. Write \(v_i\) for the column missing axis \(i\). For any \(i\ne j\), the entries of \(v_i,v_j\) on their two common coordinates are proportional, by the Fano description in the quotient by axis \(i\). Scale the first three coordinates so that \(v_4=(1,1,1,0)\). Scaling the other columns gives \(v_1=(0,1,1,a),v_2=(1,0,1,b),v_3=(1,1,0,c)\). The common-coordinate ratios imply \(a=b=c\ne0\). Scale the last coordinate. The original dictionary is therefore projectively equivalent to the eight odd-parity binary vectors.

**Which targets can that cube decode?** First consider a target proportional to a cube point, normalized to that point. Every four-point affine plane containing it gives an identity, and every plane missing it gives zero, using binary functionals extended to the field. On any missing plane, each pair of points lies in a plane through the target. Each side of the decoder on the missing plane therefore has rank at least two. The zero product forces both ranks to be exactly two. Any two distinct missing planes intersect in two points, since there is just one missing plane in each parallel class. Their left spans coincide because the common pair is independent. Their union over all missing planes comprises all seven nontarget points. In a plane through the target the three nontarget columns must be independent, a contradiction.

For a target off all column lines, the even-class argument applies with \(d=4\). Each class has exactly two columns. Three would place three binary cube points on a projective line, which is impossible even after extending scalars. Thus at least two distinct binary secant lines intersect in the target. Intersections of binary linear subspaces commute with scalar extension by Gaussian elimination, so the target is projectively binary. The only remaining binary points are the seven even-parity directions. These all lie in one three-dimensional linear subspace and cannot include four independent targets. This excludes eight terms in every characteristic-two field.

Nine terms suffice over every field by the project's tensor-square construction for two symbols. The exact binary disjoint-recovery parameter \(P(4,4)=9\) is classical. The new issue here is that unrestricted riffle decoders can overlap and can have coefficients anywhere in the chosen field. The exhaustive two-element check covers the entire eight-column search. The extension-field checks cover the normalized cube's 85 and 585 projective target directions, respectively. The arbitrary-extension proof above is mathematical and awaits end-to-end formalization.
