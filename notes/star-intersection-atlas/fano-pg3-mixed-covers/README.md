# Which witnesses force another covering pair on Fano and PG(2,3)?

On the seven-point Fano plane, **105 tests already rule out three covering pairs**. On the thirteen-point plane PG(2,3), four pairs pass all 520 tests of the same kind. Requiring those *same* pairs to pass the tests with points and lines interchanged raises the minimum to **exactly five**. The difference identifies where information from both sides of the incidence matrix becomes necessary.

| Witnesses the pairs must cover | Fano | PG(2,3) |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Canonical and row/singleton witnesses | 4 | 4 |
| Canonical, row/singleton and column/singleton witnesses | 4 | 5 |
| All semifilters, including ones outside these families | 5 | 6 or 7 |

The first two rows are the subject of this note. The last row gives the separately obtained bounds for the full problem. The Fano full minimum uses a five-gate construction and an independent exhaustive exclusion of four even with feedback. On PG(2,3), the full range uses the previously reported six-gate lower bound for circuits with feedback and the seven-gate ordinary construction. The connection between full covers and least-fixed-point circuits is Theorem 30 of Cavalar and Oliveira. ## What is being tested?

Let $U$ be the set of nonincident point–line pairs. Write $R_p$ and $C_l$ for the subsets of $U$ in row $p$ and column $l$. Two nonempty subsets $A,B$ generate the semifilter consisting of all subsets of $U$ containing $A$ or $B$. An operand pair $E,H$ covers this witness when $E$ and $H$ belong to the semifilter but their intersection does not. Equivalently, one generator is contained only in $E$ and the other only in $H$, in either order. The operands may overlap.

At an incidence $(p,l)$, the canonical test uses $R_p,C_l$. The row/singleton test uses $R_p$ and one negative cell of column $l$. The column/singleton test uses $C_l$ and one negative cell of row $p$. A single list of operand pairs must cover all the prescribed tests. Fano contributes 21 canonical tests and 84 of each singleton type. PG(2,3) contributes 52 canonical tests and 468 of each singleton type. [Formal definitions](lean/FusionMixed.lean)

## Why 105 Fano tests suffice

Given three proposed pairs, assign each negative trace a three-coordinate word. A coordinate records exclusive containment in one operand, exclusive containment in the other, or a wildcard. These words describe faces of a three-dimensional cube. Two generators are covered precisely when their faces are disjoint. A negative singleton contained in a trace has a face meeting the trace's face.

The one-sided tests force the seven row faces to be incomparable. For distinct points $p,q$, choose a line through $p$ missing $q$. The negative cell $(q,l)$ has a face meeting the face of $R_q$ and disjoint from that of $R_p$. Reversing the points gives the other direction. There are 2,260 possible seven-element antichains of row faces.

For each antichain the certificate gives weights in $\{-1,0,1\}$ on the 21 unordered point pairs. Their sum is one. A triple is eligible as a line only if there is a column face disjoint from its three point faces and a compatible negative-cell face for each exterior point. The total weight of the three point pairs on **every eligible triple** is at most zero. In Fano every unordered pair of points lies on exactly one line. Summing over the seven actual lines would therefore give both one and a number at most zero. This contradiction rules out three pairs. Four explicit pairs pass both mixed families. ## Where the Fano argument stops

On PG(2,3), four explicit pairs cover all 52 canonical and all 468 row/singleton tests. They pass **406 of 468** column/singleton tests. For example, columns 0 and 1 have the same profile, and none of the pairs covers the witness generated by $C_1$ and the negative singleton $(0,0)$ over the incidence $(0,1)$. This is a counterexample to extending the one-sided obstruction. Three pairs cannot pass even the one-sided tests here. The thirteen row faces must be incomparable, while the three-cube face poset has width twelve. [Four-pair operands and the missing witness](lean/FusionRowMixedCounterexample.lean) · [Partial-word width argument](https://github.com/open-goat/pequalnp-proofs/discussions/19729#discussioncomment-19913262)

Five explicit pairs cover all 988 mixed tests. For this particular construction the semifilter generated by the two negative singletons $(0,3)$ and $(1,1)$ over the incidence $(0,1)$ is still uncovered. This gives a concrete way to see what the mixed tests miss. [Five-pair construction](lean/MixedFive.lean)

## Why four pairs cannot pass both sides on PG(2,3)

With four pairs, the thirteen row traces and thirteen column traces give two antichains of faces of the four-cube. At each nonincidence $(q,l)$ a single face for that negative cell must meet both its row face and its column face. It must also avoid the four row faces of points incident with $l$ and the four column faces of lines through $q$. Allowing a fresh face satisfying these conditions at each negative cell only relaxes the problem. Excluding the relaxation excludes arbitrary operands.

The shared orbit generator fixes the singleton vertices of a row family up to the 384 cube symmetries and enumerates incomparable remaining faces in their stabilizer. The fourteen strata contain 23,823,975 orbits. Weighting by orbit size gives 9,107,560,888 labeled antichains. A separate count by face dimension agrees in every stratum. [Orbit generator](search/mixed_family_generator.cpp) · [Independent count](search/mixed_count_check.cpp) · [Counts by stratum](search/expected.tsv)

Two geometric searches read that same orbit stream. One constructs lines by covering the 78 unordered pairs of points exactly once. The other constructs the lines through one point, then checks the possible completions using orthogonal Latin squares. Both retain the same 194 row families. Those families give 478 labeled line designs. Of these, 85 admit the necessary column antichain, and **zero** admit a simultaneous row, column and negative-cell assignment. Two final implementations check the simultaneous condition, one by projective labeling and one by assigning signed words. Padding a shorter list with empty pairs also rules out smaller budgets.
