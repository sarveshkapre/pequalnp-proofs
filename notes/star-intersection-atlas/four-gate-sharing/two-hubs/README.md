# Two branching vertices and the limits of rectangle counting

In the row/column-star model, arbitrary unions are free and each binary intersection costs one. A circuit may reuse a gate. A formula pays for each occurrence. A **single conjunction** here means an intersection of free star unions. Its cost is the number of clauses minus one. This restriction specifies the formula shape, independently of how a binary conjunction is balanced.

* A tree with exactly two branching vertices, both of degree three, has a formula of cost at most five whenever it has a circuit of cost at most four. The same implication holds for every caterpillar, with no degree bound.
* Every tree with at most twelve nonleaf vertices has a five-intersection formula, regardless of its circuit cost. At most eleven nonleaf vertices suffice to guarantee the same bound using a single conjunction. Eleven is sharp for that guarantee.
* For every even t ≥ 4, there is an explicit two-branch tree on which a single conjunction costs **exactly t+1** intersections. A free union of two conjunctions costs **at most t**. At t=4 the tree has 16 vertices and its exact unrestricted circuit and formula costs are both four. These examples demonstrate a limitation of rectangle counting as a lower bound for unrestricted formulas.

It remains open whether *any* tree has circuit cost four and formula cost six. After removing duplicate leaves, a witness would have at most 32 vertices in total, at least 13 nonleaves, and could not be a caterpillar. If it had exactly two branching vertices, at least one would have degree four or more. Removing duplicate leaves preserves both costs by copying that vertex's memberships in each input set.

## What a rectangle cover proves

The nonedges excluded by one free union of stars form an arbitrary rectangle. An intersection of d such unions therefore represents a graph exactly when d rectangles cover its nonedges. In particular, the relation “row code is a subset of column code” on d binary coordinates has a formula with d−1 paid intersections. Coordinate i excludes the rectangle of rows containing i and columns missing i. Row and column labels may differ.

For a finite poset P write G(P) for its reflexive order matrix, with one row and one column per element. The minimum number of rectangles covering its nonorder entries equals its **2-dimension**, the smallest d such that P embeds as an induced subposet in the Boolean lattice on d coordinates. A cube embedding gives the d rectangles just described. Conversely, for a nonorder rectangle A × B, the upward closure U of A misses B, so U × (P minus U) extends that rectangle and is still nonorder. Membership in these upward closures gives an order-reflecting embedding. This classical connection is part of the rectangle-cover literature. [Rectangle covering and poset 2-dimension](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/download/v24i2p37/pdf/)

Orient the edges of a bipartite tree S from one color class to the other to obtain a height-at-most-two poset. Its reflexive order graph G(P) is precisely S with one private leaf attached at every vertex. Off-diagonal entries give the original tree and diagonal entries attach the leaves. If S is the subtree induced by the nonleaves of T, then T is obtained from this graph by deleting unused private leaves and duplicating others. Restricting or copying the raw star memberships preserves any formula upper bound. Reversing the orientation transposes the graph. Trees with fewer than three vertices admit a raw free union on their own shores.

## An infinite two-branch separation

Use t=2h ≥ 4 and W = binomial(t,h). Make a poset with minimal elements p, x1, x2 and maximal elements q, y1, …, y(W−2). Its strict relations are p < q, p < every y, and both x < q. Its reflexive order graph is a tree on 2(W+2) vertices with exactly two branching vertices.

**A formula using t gates.** On the first t coordinates choose a word w of size h−1. Assign all W middle-sized words, without repetition, to the x and y elements. Give the two x elements words that do not contain w. Such a choice exists even at t=4. For the first subset relation, use each assigned word on both sides of its element. Give row p and column q the word w. Give row q the full set and column p the empty set. This includes the W assigned diagonals, p → q, and some allowed p → y entries. For the second subset relation use two bits. Its two complete bipartite components are row p to columns p and all y, and rows q and both x to column q. Realize the components by the singleton codes {1} and {2}, giving inactive rows the full set and inactive columns the empty set. The free union of the two relations gives precisely the target. They cost t−1 and one paid intersection respectively.

**One conjunction costs at least t+1 gates.** Suppose that P embeds using t+1 coordinates. Its label for p is nonempty because p is incomparable with each x. Above p lie W−1 incomparable maxima, including q. If p used two or more bits, the remaining lattice would have width at most binomial(t−1,h−1)=W/2, by Sperner's theorem. Hence p is a singleton. Delete its bit from the maxima. They are now a (W−1)-element antichain in the t-cube.

We give the small stability argument needed here. This antichain has at most h members outside rank h. Indeed the Lubell inequality, obtained by counting maximal chains, charges a middle element 1/W and any other element at least (1+1/h)/W. If k members were outside the middle, they would forbid at most k+1 middle members, since W−1−k middle members are present. Each outlier is comparable to at least h+1 middle members. If k>0 this forces k=h and exactly the same h+1 forbidden middle members for every outlier. Only ranks h−1 and h+1 can have exactly h+1 middle neighbors. Distinct sets in rank h−1 have different upper shadows, since the intersection of that shadow recovers the set. The dual statement holds in rank h+1. The two types of shadows differ as well: the upper shadow of an (h−1)-set has union the entire ground set, whereas the lower shadow of an (h+1)-set has union of size h+1 < 2h. Thus two distinct outliers cannot share a shadow, contradicting k=h ≥ 2. All maxima consequently belong to rank h, apart from the deleted singleton coordinate.

Let Q be the remaining label of q. Each x misses the p bit and is contained in Q. Any proper subset of Q belongs to at least h+1 middle words. At most one middle word is missing, so at least two of our maxima contain it. An x may lie beneath q alone. This forces both x labels to equal Q, an impossibility. Thus at least t+2 coordinates, or t+1 intersection gates in a single conjunction, are necessary.

**Matching upper bound for one conjunction.** Adjoin two bits α, β to the t payload bits. Assign p = {α}, q = {α,β,1,2}, and x1 = {β,1}, x2 = {β,2}. Give the y elements {α} together with distinct h-subsets of the payload that are not contained in {1,2}. Enough choices exist, including when h=2. These labels realize exactly the order.

At t=4 the four-gate construction is optimal even among circuits. The checker enumerates all three-gate wirings: in the zero-cell formulation each paid gate joins two meets, each consisting of a raw rectangle and any subset of earlier gate outputs. A final free rectangle clips rows or columns, which receive the all-zero word. The output taps include the last gate after padding shorter circuits by an idempotent paid join. An unused earlier gate is allowed. Swap symmetric arms and rename their raw rectangles. This gives 120 wiring/output combinations, 16 distinct truth tables and 14 distinct labeled lists of host neighborhoods. Exhaustive domain search on all row/column assignments excludes the 8×8 target in every host. It also accepts realizable controls, including the size-six matching in an appropriate host.

## Two uniform five-gate bounds

**Up to twelve nonleaves.** The replay independently generates all unordered rooted trees by multisets of child trees and identifies unrooted trees by their minimum canonical rooting. It finds 235 shapes at order eleven and 551 at order twelve. At order eleven every core has an explicit six-coordinate induced poset embedding. At order twelve, 547 have such certificates and four have explicit formulas of the other shape above with t=5. Every cell of each certificate is checked. Smaller cores extend to order eleven and then restrict back. Hence any fully leaf-decorated tree in the five exceptional four-gate hosts also has a five-gate formula: its diagonals are a matching, so its core has at most eleven vertices by the [previous matching bound](../spiders/README.md). The low-order result applies to arbitrary trees with these core sizes, without a four-gate assumption.

**Eleven is sharp for a single conjunction of six clauses.** Use the same double-star poset with eight y elements and two x elements (12 core vertices). The seven-bit construction above works with five payload bits and h=2. In six bits, p must be a singleton because nine incomparable maxima lie above it. Normalize p to the first bit. For a q label of rank 2, 3, 4 or 5, enumerate all incomparable pairs of nonempty x labels contained in q and missing p. For each pair, compute the largest antichain of y labels containing p, incomparable with q, and containing neither x. The maximum possible numbers of y elements are respectively 0, 3, 7, 6, where 0 denotes no admissible pair. Rank one leaves no room for the x elements and rank six would make q universal. Eight y elements are impossible. The exhibited tree therefore needs exactly six paid gates in a single conjunction, although a five-gate formula exists.

**Every caterpillar.** The [previous exclusion certificates](../spiders/evidence.json) explicitly exclude the 21-edge path from all five exceptional hosts (the one-arm profile [21]). In a caterpillar with at least two nonleaves its nonleaves form a path. Extending that path by a leaf at each end shows it has at most 19 vertices in those hosts. Here is a 19-element induced fence in the six-coordinate Boolean lattice, expressed as bit masks:

    7 23 22 30 28 29 25 27 11 43 42 46 44 45 37 53 49 51 50

Strict inclusion occurs exactly from an even-indexed word to an adjacent odd-indexed word, starting at index zero. Its reflexive order matrix gives one six-clause formula for a fully leaf-decorated 19-vertex path. Restriction and duplication cover every caterpillar with such a spine. The other four-gate wirings already admit five-gate formulas.

## Exactly two branching vertices of degree three

After removing duplicate leaves, either the preceding [one-branch theorem](../spiders/README.md) applies, or each branching vertex has two outward arms. Write d for the length of the path joining the two vertices and (a,b), (c,e) for their outward arm lengths, sorted within and between the two pairs. A hub cannot have two arms of length one in a reduced tree. The 21-edge path exclusion bounds each of a+b, c+e and a+d+c by 20. The matching number is

    floor(a/2)+floor(b/2)+floor(c/2)+floor(e/2) + floor((d+1+u+v)/2),

where u and v indicate whether the corresponding pair has an odd arm. Match inside the arms first, excluding the hubs. Matching a hub into an even arm gains nothing and can be omitted. At each end an odd arm supplies one possible extra pendant vertex to match. Optimizing on the resulting path on d+1+u+v vertices gives the expression. Its value is at most 11 in the exceptional hosts. These necessary conditions leave 4,156 profiles.

The certificate covers all 4,156 with 476 explicit formulas on larger profiles and 182 excluded smaller profiles, using arm truncation at fixed hub distance and allowing reversal. Positive certificates evaluate row and column masks on the entire graph. Each negative certificate picks a matching with specified absent cross edges. Every target has a nonneighbor at each vertex. The [published normalization](../spiders/README.md) therefore forces distinct maximal pairs with both endpoints positive in the scalar zero-cell function. The previous C++ checker exhausts all assignments in each of the five hosts, giving 910 exclusions. This proves the subcubic two-branch claim at all sizes.

## Replay and credit

From this directory, with Python 3.10+ and Clang or GCC, run the following. The C++ source is the one in the adjacent pinned spiders package.

```sh
c++ -std=c++17 -O3 -Wall -Wextra -pedantic ../spiders/matching_check.cpp -o /tmp/tree-matching-check
python3 -I -B -u replay.py /tmp/tree-matching-check
```

The program uses only the standard library. It checks both family constructions on 927,868 cells across t = 4, 5, 6, 8, 10 and 12. The even-parameter theorem uses the written proof for all t. The finite lower bounds and profile certificates have executable replays. There is no Lean or official-verifier verdict for this package.
