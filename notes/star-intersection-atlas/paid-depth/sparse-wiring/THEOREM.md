# Sharp complementation on sparse random wiring

Fix a paid target depth D ≥ 2 and put m = 2^(D−1). Use pjscruggs’s model of free row stars, column stars and unions, with one unit charged per binary intersection. The source has k paid gates. Raw inputs, finite grid dimensions and the output union are unrestricted. A source with feedback is evaluated at its least fixed point. The target is an acyclic formula.

**Depth two.** Sample each unordered pair of gates independently with probability λ/k, direct it from the earlier to the later gate, and place it on one arm of the reader. For any fixed 0 ≤ λ ≤ 57/40, with probability tending to one the worst depth-two complement on the sampled wiring costs Θ(2^(k/2)). This holds simultaneously for all arm placements, raw inputs, outputs and adversarial deletions of sampled wires. The lower bound allows the target to share wires. At λ = 57/40 the largest connected component contains *more than half* the gates with high probability. The upper bound therefore crosses the point where putting whole components in two equal groups ceases to work.

**Arbitrary correlation between arms.** More generally each ordered pair independently receives a random subset of the reader’s arms. The laws may vary by pair. It suffices that the sum of the two arm-presence probabilities on each pair is at most λ/k. The same bounds hold, including at 57/40 and under thinning, even if both arms always receive a wire together. Thus per-arm probability at most 57/(80k) suffices at depth two. If every wire occupies both arms, distributivity already reduces each gate to a free union of its predecessors and its own raw intersection, so that extreme case flattens to one layer at any density. The coupling handles mixtures that need not flatten. The sandwich argument below uses an absent wire to offset the cost of a double-arm wire.

**Every fixed depth.** For λ > 1 let ρ be the positive solution of ρ = 1−exp(−λρ), put b = λ exp(−λ), and δ = log₂(4/3). A sufficient condition for sharp worst cost Θ_D(2^(k/m)) with high probability is

    ρ [1 − δ (b/2 − b²/6)] < 1/m.

In particular it holds on an interval extending strictly beyond λ_m = −m log(1−1/m), the point where the giant reaches fraction 1/m. Every smaller edge probability follows by coupling to a thinning of the same wiring. The statements concern typical wirings and worst choices of data on each wiring. They do not identify a hardness transition or bound the expected cost on rare wirings.

## A finite criterion uniform over outputs

Let a component of the undirected wiring skeleton have s gates. Mark h vertex-disjoint pairs (v,p) inside it. The gate v has two raw-only arms, no designated status is assumed, and its only potential use is on one arm of p. The other gates may be wired arbitrarily inside their respective components, including cycles. Let B = ceil(k/m). Suppose every other component has at most L gates and there are at least m(L−1) singleton components outside the marked component, for an integer L ≥ 1. Then

    s − δh ≤ B  implies  complement formula cost ≤ m·2^B + m−1.

For a fixed orientation choosing one arm at every gate, start from the designated output gates and follow the selected arms backwards to all their paid predecessors. Require absence of all raw signals on the selected arm of every reached gate. These requirements specify one rectangle of cells. They certify rejection because the complement of the reached set is closed under firing at those cells. Conversely, at a rejecting cell choose a false arm at each false gate of the least fixed point. The reachability search stays among false gates and all its raw tests pass. Raw output stars impose a common row and column restriction throughout. This is the established orientation-certificate argument; the [Boolean-rank work](https://github.com/open-goat/pequalnp-proofs/discussions/19729#discussioncomment-19919194) and its [field-rank extension](https://github.com/open-goat/pequalnp-proofs/discussions/19729#discussioncomment-19920366) study the resulting full traces. Here the aim is to decide which tests can be placed in different target layers.

A marked gate v which is itself designated must be false. Factor off that requirement as a union of two rectangles and replace v by false on its reader’s arm. This replacement preserves output rejection: its value is determined by raw signals alone, and on cells satisfying the factored requirement it was false already. A deleted leaf wire likewise separates off a one-gate or vacuous factor. Write t for the number of peeled gates. The remaining h−t pairs each have three choices. Either p selects its other arm, or it selects the arm containing v and selects one of the two arms of v. If p is never reached its choice has no effect. In the residual diagram, which parents are reached depends only on orientations outside the private leaves since those leaves have no predecessors. For every assignment of the other gate orientations this coding enumerates all rejection rectangles. Thus the marked component has one core factor of at most

    2^(s−t−2(h−t)) 3^(h−t)

rectangles, together with at most t separate two-rectangle factors. The [Lean theorem](../../../../lean/PEqualNP/PaidDepth/LeafTraces.lean) proves that the residual predicate, for arbitrary base row and column tests and arbitrary parent demand sets depending on the residual choices, is representable by precisely this many rectangle slots. Repeated and empty slots are allowed.

Give a factor with N rectangle slots integer weight ceil(log₂ N). The core weight is at most ceil(s−t−δ(h−t)), hence at most B and at most s−t. Treat each peeled factor as weight one. All other components of size j allow at most 2^j rectangles by orienting their gates, so assign them weight j. The total integer weight is at most k. Place the core in one of m bins of capacity B. Reserve the singleton components. Place the remaining factors of weight at most L wherever they fit. Failure on an item of weight l would leave total free capacity at most m(l−1), yet the item and the reserved units must still fit in that total capacity by the total weight bound. Finally distribute the reserved units. The [Lean packing theorem](../../../../lean/PEqualNP/PaidDepth/ReservePacking.lean) constructs this assignment for arbitrary integer bin capacities, including the residual capacities after placing the core. Omit any zero-weight factors from the packing and absorb their single rectangle into one group. Within each bin intersecting factors distributes to a union of at most 2^B rectangles, each obtainable by one paid intersection. Intersect the m resulting unions in a balanced tree. The argument also covers a growing L as long as the stated singleton supply is available.

For a ten-gate example, let three private raw-only gates each feed a different reader on one arm. Wire the three readers in a directed cycle via their other arms and leave four more gates isolated. The main component has size six and the bin capacity is five. Designating all three readers and none of the private gates produces exactly 27 distinct orientation traces in the core; they fit in five bits. For every other output selection the same two-bin bound applies after peeling designated leaves. The replay checks all 64 core outputs and all 4,096 raw assignments there.

The lower bound uses the bitwise inequality construction of Cavalar and Oliveira. Designate every gate and give its raw arms the bit-mismatch test in its own coordinate. On equal strings all gates stay false. Any unequal coordinate fires immediately irrespective of wiring. The complement is a matching on 2^k labels, which needs Ω_D(2^(k/m)) paid intersections even with target sharing by the [fixed-depth matching bound](../THEOREM.md). Thus the cost scale is attained on every fixed wiring.

## Counting usable pairs in the giant component

We use the classical supercritical Erdős–Rényi theorem at fixed λ > 1: the largest component has (ρ+o(1))k gates in probability, and the second largest has o(k) gates in probability. For the calculation below take a fixed component-size threshold εk with 0 < ε < ρ. With high probability only the giant exceeds it. Singleton components number (exp(−λ)+o(1))k in probability by the first and second moments of the isolation indicators. [Bollobás, Janson and Riordan, Theorems 3.1 and 3.6](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0504589)

For each possible parent v in a component of at least εk gates, let A_v be its number of degree-one neighbors with smaller index. Select one such neighbor for each parent with A_v > 0. In a component of more than two vertices, these are vertex-disjoint pairs. Write X_r for the sum of binom(A_v,r) over eligible parents. Pointwise, the pair count h satisfies h ≥ X₁ − X₂.

For fixed r = 1,2, the r earlier leaves and their parent can be selected in binom(k,r+1) ways: the largest selected index is the parent. The probability of precisely the required leaf incidences is

    (λ/k)^r (1−λ/k)^[r(k−r−1) + binom(r,2)].

All edges on the remaining k−r vertices, including the parent, retain their independent Bernoulli law. The size of the parent’s component in that remaining graph must be at least εk−r, so conditional eligibility has probability ρ+o(1) by exchangeability and the giant theorem. Consequently E[X_r]/k → ρ b^r/(r+1)!.

For concentration, expand the second moment in pairs of marked stars. Overlap contributes O(k): a compatible overlapping pair has a connected union with v vertices and at least v−1 distinct required edges, hence at most O(k^v k^(−v+1)) in total for each fixed shape. For vertex-disjoint stars, the two specified incidence probabilities multiply up to a factor 1+O(1/k) (only a bounded number of absent edges are counted twice). Removing the 2r leaves leaves an independent graph on k−2r vertices. Conditional joint eligibility tends to ρ². Indeed, both distinct specified parents must lie in the unique giant with probability tending to one conditional on eligibility, and by exchangeability their joint giant-membership probability tends to ρ² since its size divided by k tends to ρ and is bounded by one. Removing or restoring a bounded number of pendant leaves cannot move any other component across the chosen fixed threshold with appreciable probability. Thus Var(X_r)=o(k²). It follows that h/k ≥ ρ(b/2−b²/6)−o(1) with high probability. The finite criterion applies with the largest other component as L (or L=1 if needed) because there are linearly many singleton components. This proves the displayed sufficient condition. At λ_m the unsubtracted giant fraction is exactly 1/m and the subtraction is positive; continuity gives a nonempty interval beyond λ_m for every fixed D.

At λ = 57/40, elementary rational bounds give 1/2 < ρ < 531/1000, b > 34/100 and δ > 2/5. The function b/2−b²/6 increases on [0,1], giving a value above 15/100. The effective giant fraction is therefore below 24957/50000 < 1/2. The [finite replay](check_sparse.py) checks these inequalities with rational Taylor-series enclosures. To thin the sampled skeleton, keep the same component bins and candidate pairs; a lost private wire gives a separated unit, which saves at least as much core weight as a surviving pair. Multiple components created by deletions may be treated as one original factor. The same counting also gives a comparison for heterogeneous two-arm laws.

## A coupling for all two-arm laws

Generate the same homogeneous skeleton H with edge probability Q = λ/k. If Q = 0 there are no wires. Otherwise, for each edge of H independently, put it in the actual wiring as a left-only, right-only or double-arm edge with respective probabilities equal to the original probabilities of these three outcomes divided by Q. Use the remaining probability for an absent edge. Each original nonempty probability sum is at most Q, so this defines the desired independent-pair law. Conditional on H the probability of an absent edge is at least that of a double-arm edge: their difference is one minus the sum of the original two arm marginals divided by Q. The ordering or bias of the single-arm choices is immaterial.

Choose the h protected pairs in H before revealing the marks, breaking ties by gate index. For the core weight, credit one saved bit for an absent edge, δ for a single-arm edge and zero for a double-arm edge. An absent edge makes the leaf an independent unit. For a single-arm edge the earlier finite bound applies whether or not its leaf is designated. Simply leave any double-arm pair in the ordinary binary enumeration. These savings hold simultaneously for every output and for every subsequent deletion of wires or arm occurrences. The core weight is bounded by the ceiling of s minus the sum of credits, while its weight together with the peeled units still does not exceed s. Outside this core the earlier bin packing is unchanged.

For each mark, with conditional absent and double probabilities u and d, its expected credit equals δ + (1−δ)u − δd ≥ δ, since u ≥ d and δ < 1/2 (indeed (4/3)² < 2). Conditional variance of the sum is at most h because the chosen edge marks are independent and lie in [0,1]. Chebyshev’s inequality therefore loses only o(k) credit with high probability, uniformly over all the allowed laws; for example an additive loss k^(2/3) has probability at most k^(−1/3). Strict slack in the displayed criterion absorbs this loss and integer rounding. The adversary may choose all raw masks and outputs after seeing both stages. The result is uniform in the probability bound for each stipulated law. It does not assert one event that handles an adversary selecting the mark law after seeing the skeleton.

## Scope and attribution

Pjscruggs supplied the project star-circuit model and atlas. Cavalar and Oliveira supplied the inequality construction. The fixed-depth matching bound, the orientation description of rejection, and the classical random-graph giant theorem are used here. The contribution is the output-uniform component packing using private gate pairs, and the resulting sharp complement bound beyond the ordinary balanced-component threshold. The unrestricted depth-two exponent remains between 1/2 and 3/5. Larger fixed mean degrees remain open in general. Historical priority for the precise new formulation has not been established.
