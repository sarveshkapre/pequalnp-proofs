# Sharp fixed-depth complementation on subdivided gate wiring

Take any finite simple graph as the wiring between the original gates. Replace each edge by a path of new paid gates, with different paths having disjoint interiors. The resulting graph describes where dependencies are allowed. Signals may travel in either direction on a link, including both directions with least-fixed-point feedback. In the single-arm model a predecessor can occur on at most one arm of a given reader. The reader can take any number of other predecessors. Every original or inserted gate can have its own arbitrary raw row and column inputs, and any gates can be outputs.

**The two-layer result.** Insert at least 49 gates on each edge. On every such wiring with k gates in total, the worst minimum size for the complement at paid depth two is Θ(2^(k/2)). The upper bound constructs a formula. The matching lower bound permits target sharing. The upper bound holds simultaneously for all single-arm placements, deletions, finite row and column universes, raw masks and output selections. The number 49 is sufficient; the smallest sufficient length is open.

In particular, start with a bounded-degree regular expander and subdivide every edge exactly 49 times. This gives leaf-free wiring of linear treewidth where the sharp complement bound still holds. Contracting the paths recovers the original graph. A bounded-degree expander has linear treewidth since a balanced separator has linear size by expansion; a tree decomposition supplies a balanced separator bag. A fixed subdivision increases the vertex count by only a constant factor. This application goes beyond the earlier sublinear-treewidth criterion. The gate budget here includes the inserted gates. [Prior separator bound](../structure/THEOREM.md) · [Examples of expander families](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02126799)

**Every fixed depth.** Put m = 2^(D−1) for target depth D ≥ 2. Let a be the smallest integer satisfying

    2^(16+3a) ≥ 3^(16m).

At least 4a+1 internal gates per edge suffice for the same conclusion Θ_D(2^(k/m)). It suffices to choose `a` disjoint groups of four interior gates and reserve one additional interior gate for the cut. Only the three links inside each chosen group need the single-arm restriction. The groups used below are consecutive along the path, and disjoint from the cut gate. Other links may use both arms. At depth two a = 12.

## An output-dependent bound

Designate the paid gates used in the final free union. Delete all occurrences of these gates from the inputs of other gates, including dependencies among designated gates. This preserves the rejection relation. A rejecting certificate is a set of gates containing the outputs, with one chosen arm at each member. Its chosen raw masks must be absent and every predecessor on a chosen arm must belong to the set. Dropping a predecessor that is already required in the set does not change this test. Such a certificate proves rejection even in the presence of feedback because the complement of the set is closed under firing. Conversely, at a rejecting cell all false gates of the least state form such a set after selecting a false arm for each. The two raw output masks impose one common rectangle restriction. This uses the existing [orientation-certificate result](https://github.com/open-goat/pequalnp-proofs/discussions/19729#discussioncomment-19919194).

Call an original edge intact if its entire replacement path still has a dependency, in at least one direction, on every link after this deletion. Write f for the number of intact paths. The construction gives

    1 + formula cost ≤ K_D · 2^(k/m) · [3 / 2^((1+3a/16)/m)]^f.

The bracket is strictly below one by the choice of a (a positive power of three is not a power of two). Consequently, on this family an output whose complement costs at least a fixed positive fraction of the worst-case scale has only boundedly many intact paths. This conclusion concerns the chosen outputs. On the same wiring other outputs can break every path.

## Two readers and seven choices

Consider three distinct gates: a nondesignated center and two neighbors. Only those neighbors may read the center. Each uses it on at most one arm. Either or both reads may be absent. Relabel the arm choices at each neighbor so that its bypass arm is zero. If both neighbors select zero, the center cannot be demanded by a selected arm. Delete it from any active certificate and set its own choice to zero. No other active gate loses a necessary predecessor. This works even when the center itself depends on other gates or lies on a cycle.

Thus three otherwise binary choices occupy at most seven slots. In a block with r other gates and h vertex-disjoint protected triples, all local rejection tests require at most 2^r 7^h rectangles. The coding works in parallel since the triples have no common gate. For fixed choices the row-only and column-only closed sets can differ: their intersection still contains every seed and is closed under the same dependencies. The [Lean theorem](../../../../lean/PEqualNP/PaidDepth/ReaderBypass.lean) proves the full rectangle representation in this abstract gate graph. It allows arbitrary extra raw predicates elsewhere.

The local constant is exact. On a three-gate diagram with both readers designated there is an explicit choice of raw row and column masks for which rejection is equality on seven labels. If just one reader now receives the center on both arms, rejection can be equality on eight labels. A rectangle contained in a diagonal covers at most one diagonal cell. The [second Lean theorem](../../../../lean/PEqualNP/PaidDepth/ReaderBypassSharp.lean) evaluates both circuits on every label pair. This shows why simply applying the seven-slot estimate to unrestricted double-arm links would be incorrect.

## Paying for a separator

Cut one interior gate on each intact path, outside its protected groups. The last interior gate suffices when the first 4a interiors are used for groups. Keep all other gates. No two original gates are now in the same remaining component. Each component is an isolated path or one hub with disjoint path arms. Order components consecutively. On a component with a hub list it first, then each arm from the hub outward. At any prefix of the order, at most two earlier vertices have an edge to a later vertex: the hub and the current tip. A path alone needs at most one. This bound is independent of the original degrees, number of components and subdivision lengths.

In each protected group of four consecutive interior gates, its two middle gates are adjacent in the normalized graph. They cannot both be designated, since all dependencies between designated gates were deleted. Choose a nondesignated middle gate as the center and its two immediate neighbors as readers. All possible reads of this center are inside the triple. In the remaining graph there are af disjoint connected triples. The chosen cut gate touches none of them as a member.

Give each such center weight 13/16 and each other remaining gate weight one. A complete triple has enough weight to pay for its seven rectangle slots since 7^16 ≤ 2^45. Total weight is W = k−f−3af/16. Cut the order at the first crossing of each jW/m for j=1,...,m−1 (allow empty blocks). Each block has weight at most W/m+1 because individual weights are at most one. Remove the union of the earlier frontiers of these cuts, of size u ≤ 2(m−1). No remaining dependency joins different blocks. Every connected triple surviving these removals lies in one block. At most u triples meet these extra separator gates. Abandoning their discounts can increase the effective weight of any block by at most 3u/16.

Give each of the f+u separator gates one of three signatures: inactive, active with its left arm, or active with its right arm. Discard inconsistent signatures. Impose the raw tests at active separator gates. Inside each block require its output gates and any predecessors required by an active separator arm. A chosen block arm that demands an inactive separator is forbidden. For fixed signature these are independent block tests. No center of a surviving triple can be such an extra seed: all its readers remain in its own triple. The seven-slot theorem therefore applies. Conversely, unioning the local closed sets with the active separator gates gives a valid global rejection certificate. A global rejecting certificate restricts to witnesses in every block. This argument is valid for the least state of cyclic sources.

For each signature intersect the m block tests in a balanced tree. Each test is a free union of at most 2^(W/m+1+3u/16) rectangles. Each rectangle costs one intersection of a row strip with a column strip. Common raw exclusions can be absorbed into those rectangles. Freely union the answers across signatures. The cost, plus one, is at most a D-dependent constant times 3^f 2^(W/m). This proves the stated output-dependent inequality. The case with no residual weight uses constant tests.

For the lower bound on every wiring, designate every gate and let its raw arms test a mismatch in its own bit. On equal labels no gate fires; on unequal labels a mismatching gate fires immediately. This realizes inequality regardless of all paid wires. Its complement is the diagonal on 2^k labels. The existing depth-D matching lower bound gives Ω_D(2^(k/m)). Pjscruggs supplied the project's star model and atlas. The lower-bound construction is due to Cavalar and Oliveira. ## Why the stability bound fails with unrestricted double-arm wiring

On any connected subdivided graph choose a spanning tree directed toward one root. Put each tree predecessor on both arms of its parent. Include all other links on a single arm, oriented consistently with a topological order ending at the root. The root is the only designated output and has no outgoing dependency to remove. Every original path remains intact. Give gate i the two raw arms for a mismatch in coordinate i. A mismatch fires its own gate and propagates along the double-arm tree all the way to the root. On equal strings no gate fires. Thus the complement is equality on all 2^k labels, at its full Ω_D(2^(k/m)) cost even when f grows linearly with k. Each nonroot gate needs only one double-arm *outgoing* link in this construction.

This rules out extending the decaying factor in f to arbitrary arm placements. The example attains the baseline and leaves open whether some other double-arm wiring can exceed it. The replay checks all label pairs on a finite cycle with one output. The bit mismatch lower bound and its attribution are as above.

## Randomly located single-arm restrictions also suffice

Fix D, a maximum original degree Δ ≥ 2 and any η > 0 with η ≤ 1. The original base graph can be any deterministic sequence of simple graphs of degree at most Δ with n tending to infinity. Independently mark each physical link with probability at least η. A marked link guarantees that a predecessor can occur on at most one arm of its reader (in either direction). Unmarked links permit arbitrary dependencies on both arms. After seeing all marks, an adversary chooses the wiring, feedback, raw masks and output gates. For a sufficiently large *fixed* number L of internal gates per base edge, the worst complement is still Θ_D,Δ,L(2^(k/m)) with probability tending to one. No random model on the base graph or its degree sequence is required.

Choose N so that Pr[Bin(N,η³)<a] ≤ 1/(8Δ), and take L=4N+1. A group of four is usable if its three internal links are marked. These events are independent across disjoint groups and paths, and each has probability at least η³. Declare a base edge bad if fewer than a of its groups are usable. Bad-edge events are independent with probabilities at most q=1/(8Δ). For example, at depth two on a cubic base with η=1/2, N=152 and L=609 suffice by an exact binomial comparison.

With high probability every component of the graph of bad base edges has O(log n) vertices and at least n/2 original vertices are isolated in that graph. For the first assertion, a component of size at least j contains a tree on j vertices. With a specified root, there are at most 4^(j−1) rooted plane tree shapes (encode the depth-first contour by its 2j−2 up/down steps), and at most Δ^(j−1) choices of neighboring vertices for a shape. Counting only genuine tree embeddings gives probability at most n(4Δq)^(j−1). Take j a sufficiently large multiple of log n. For the second assertion the expected number of bad edges is at most n/16 and its variance is at most its mean. With high probability at most n/4 bad edges touch at most n/2 vertices.

Normalize the chosen outputs as above. Cut one gate on each intact good path; for each such path retain the required a triples from its marked groups. Write f for this number of cuts. Any component left after the cuts that contains original vertices can connect them only along bad paths. It has at most (1+ΔL) times the size of a bad-base component in vertices. A component with no original vertex occupies at most one path. Moreover each of the at least n/2 original vertices isolated in the bad-base graph belongs to its own component of size at most K=1+ΔL. These facts hold simultaneously for all adversarial choices; absent links only split components.

The weight W = k−f−3af/16 is now spread over components of weight O(log n), with Ω(n) weight in components of weight at most K. Reserve those small components. Assign the larger components whole to m bins of capacity W/m. Failure to place one would leave only O(m log n) total free space, whereas all reserved weight is still unassigned. Put each reserved component into a currently least loaded bin; the final maximum weight is at most W/m+K. No extra separator gates are needed. The signature argument and lower bound now give the claimed sharp order for all permitted outputs and masks. The asymptotic random assertion is a written proof.

For unrestricted acyclic sources the [depth-two exponent](../structure/UNRESTRICTED.md) remains between 1/2 and 3/5. The case of an unsubdivided cubic core also remains open. A single double-arm link defeats the local seven-slot lemma; whether every long subdivision satisfies the global theorem under arbitrary double-arm placement remains open here.
